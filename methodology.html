How the score is built

The full picture behind the 0–100 number — what signals we pull, how we normalize them, and why the weights are a starting point, not gospel.

The formula

Every business gets a single score that is a weighted average of three normalized sub-scores:

Score =  (wR × Reputation + wS × Search + wQ × SiteQuality)  ÷ (wR + wS + wQ)

Default:  wR = 50,  wS = 30,  wQ = 20  → weights must sum to 100 for the scale to hold

Dividing by the sum of weights means the formula stays correct even when you adjust sliders to values that don't sum to 100 — the relative weighting is always preserved.

The weighting is a judgment call, not objective truth. Anyone can argue a different split and not be wrong. That's why the leaderboard exposes the sliders — show how rankings shift rather than presenting one number as the final answer.

Weight rationale

Reputation
50%
Search Visibility

Reputation is heaviest because it's the cleanest data, what customers look at first, and the cheapest to pull. Search is second because local pack position directly drives foot traffic — but rank shifts by hundreds of meters, so we standardize the search origin. Site quality is last because for most local service businesses (plumbers, HVAC, electricians) the website barely determines whether someone hires them.

Reputation sub-score

Combines three signals, each normalized to 0–100 before blending:

SignalWeightHow it's normalized
Google rating55% Floor at 3.0: (rating − 3.0) / 2.0 × 100. A 5.0 → 100, a 3.0 → 0, below 3.0 → 0.
Review count30% Log-scaled: log10(count) / log10(max_count) × 100. Prevents a chain with 10 000 reviews from dominating a business with 200.
Review recency15% % of reviews posted in the last 12 months × 100. Stops stale 2019 averages from looking current.
Reputation = 0.55 × ratingNorm + 0.30 × log10(count) / log10(max) × 100 + 0.15 × recentPct × 100

Search Visibility sub-score

Local pack position is the dominant signal — it's what drives phone calls. Keyword rank provides a secondary anchor.

SignalWeightMapping
Local pack position60% #1 → 100, #2 → 82, #3 → 65, #4 → 48, #5 → 35, #6–10 → 25, beyond 10 → 10. All measurements taken from the same city-center GPS point.
Avg keyword rank40% max(0, 100 − (rank − 1) × 3.5). Rank 1 → 100, rank 29+ → ~0.
Why single-point, not geo-grid? A geo-grid heatmap shows where a business ranks across a city — useful for a business's own SEO diagnosis. For a leaderboard we need relative position: everyone measured from the same point with the same keyword is consistent even if not "true" everywhere. Save geo-grid spend for deep-dives.

Site Quality sub-score

SignalWeightSource
PageSpeed score50%Google PageSpeed Insights API (free). Uses the performance score, 0–100.
Site audit score30%Ubersuggest site audit — broken links, missing meta tags, duplicate content, etc.
Mobile usability20%PageSpeed mobile score (separate run).
SiteQuality = 0.50 × pageSpeed + 0.30 × auditScore + 0.20 × mobileScore

Minimum review threshold

Businesses with fewer than 10 reviews are excluded from the ranked list rather than given a misleading score. With a small sample, a single fake or outlier review can swing a 5-star rating by half a point. The threshold is adjustable on the leaderboard — raising it tightens the list; lowering it to 0 includes everyone.
